🔥 Key Highlights

What the data reveals

  • $22.86M revenue across 4 years (2013–2016) with a healthy 43.4% profit margin.
  • N/A brand dominates sales — most stock items lack brand assignment.
  • Top customer drives a disproportionate share — heavy customer concentration risk.
  • Q4 spikes suggest strong holiday seasonality.

⚠️ Outliers Detected

Statistically extreme records

  • 2,326 sales (8.8%) exceed the IQR upper fence of $2,318. Max single sale: $21,838 vs median $260 — 84× typical.
  • 566 transactions (2.1%) have negative profit — discounted, returned or mis-priced.
  • Quantity spikes up to 360 units per line — bulk orders skew averages.
  • Top 5 highest-value invoices are the same SKU @ $1,899 × 10 — a high-ticket recurring product.

🕳️ Missing Values

Data quality gaps

  • 9,077 of 26,397 sales (34.4%) have Customer Key = 0 — anonymous walk-ins.
  • 605 of 673 items (90%) missing Brand; 312 (46%) missing Color; 656 (97%) missing Barcode.
  • 3,734 of 13,028 cities (28.7%) report population = 0.
  • 13 invoices missing Delivery Date / Duration — undelivered or cancelled.

📌 Conclusion

The business is profitable and growing, but the data warehouse hides material risks behind aggregate numbers. A third of sales are anonymous, nearly all stock items lack brand metadata, and ~9% of orders are statistical outliers distorting averages. Before trusting customer segmentation or product analytics, the team should (1) backfill Customer Key = 0 records, (2) normalize Brand / Color / Barcode in DimStockItem, and (3) flag the 566 negative-profit transactions for pricing review. Reporting median-based KPIs alongside means will give a far more honest picture of the typical sale.
